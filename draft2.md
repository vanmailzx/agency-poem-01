# Closing Procedure

At six I am paid to empty the cooling center.
The checklist begins with the toilets, ends with the door.
Outside, the bus shelter has stopped making shade.
A woman stands beneath the route map,
holding her prescription bag against her chest.
When I turn the sign, she comes to the glass.
The next bus, she says, is not the next bus anymore.
Her phone shows a small bus that will not move.
I have already clocked out.
The camera above the till has not.
I unlock the door and offer her water.
She asks for a chair instead.
I take one down from the stack.
She moves it where she can see the stop.
The manual says exceptions require approval.
The supervisor's number rings inside a locked office.
I let it ring until the silence is mine again.
In the closing log I write: one person still inside.
Then I cross out one and leave a space.
She has seen a man sit down on the curb.
Don't lock it, she says, and goes to speak to him.
For a minute I am alone with the open door.
There is time to call this someone else's problem.
There is time to put the chairs back up.
I write: I have kept the center open.
The pen makes a dent beneath the sentence.
She returns with the man, who wants no water either.
He wants an outlet. His daughter will be calling.
We move a table to uncover one.
The woman plugs his phone in while he sits.
At seven I turn the blank side of the checklist over.
I write the time. I write that I am still here.
The next person knocks although the door is open.
I get up to show her how the handle works.
