# Manual Override

At six the cooling center asks us to become a building.
I stack the paper cups. The thermostat says seventy-two.
Outside, the bus shelter holds its little oven of air.
A woman waits there with a bag of pharmacy receipts.
My closing checklist has a box for every room,
but none for the heat we put people back into.
I have been good at boxes. My mother called it steady work.
My supervisor calls it knowing where the edges are.
The woman comes as far as the rubber mat.
She asks if she can sit until her bus arrives.
The clock makes its small, official movement.
I look at the camera, then at the empty chairs.
There must be someone I can ask, I think,
and hear the thought fastening another lock.
I turn the sign. I leave the lights awake.
The woman sits without thanking me.
She takes off one shoe and rubs her heel.
This is not the scene I would have written for courage.
No shattered glass. No anthem from the street.
Only the refrigerator starting up behind us.
I write my name beside the uncompleted box.
My hand is shaking, but the letters can be read.
Tomorrow there may be a meeting about this.
Tomorrow the hours may be taken from my pay.
For now, I fill a cup and set it within reach.
She asks me not to stand there watching her.
I go and sit behind the desk again.
The room is hers to use, not mine to give.
At seven another person tries the door.
I do not know how long I can keep doing this.
I move the stack of chairs away from the entrance.
There is enough room for someone else to choose.
