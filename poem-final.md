# Closing Procedure

At six I am paid to empty the cooling center.
The checklist begins with the toilets, ends with the door.
Outside, a woman shifts from foot to foot
in the strip of shade beneath the route map.
When I turn the sign, she comes to the glass.
The next bus, she says, is not the next bus anymore.
On her phone the blue bus sits between two stops.
I have already clocked out.
The camera above the desk is still on the clock.
Last week I signed the warning about unauthorized hours.
I unlock the door and offer her water.
She asks for a chair instead.
I take one down. She moves it near the window
where she can watch the stop without standing.
The manual says exceptions require approval.
I call. Through the office door I hear
the supervisor's phone vibrating on his desk.
In the closing log I write: one person still inside.
The woman knocks against the window with her ring.
A man has lowered himself onto the curb.
Don't lock it, she says, and goes to speak to him.
I hold the pen above the box marked SECURED.
The warning waits, folded in my locker.
I need next week's hours. I leave the box blank.
Under it I write: I am keeping the center open.
They come in slowly. The man asks for an outlet.
His daughter will be calling. No, he doesn't need to sit.
I move the table. He plugs his own phone in.
The woman asks for paper and the thick black marker.
She writes OPEN, holds it up against the glass.
For how long? she asks.
My sister has my son. I have to ask her.
While the phone rings, I can hear him in her kitchen
explaining something that has happened to a spoon.
She says eight is fine, but not eight-thirty.
Eight, I tell the woman. She uncaps the marker.
Below her first word, in smaller letters,
she makes room for what I said.
